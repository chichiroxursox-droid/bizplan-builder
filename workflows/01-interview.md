# 01 — Interview

Goal: understand the *outcome* first, then the economics. One question at a time,
conversational. Accept rough ranges. Write answers into `intake/interview.md` as you go.
Mark anything they *don't* know as `[ASK]` so research can chase it. The first time a tag
comes up in conversation, explain it in one plain sentence.

## How to run it: fill the template, don't recite the list
The levers below are your **scorecard, not your script**. Your job is to fill
`intake/interview.md` with the *fewest questions possible*:

- **Open wide first.** After Q0 and Q1, ask one broad invitation: *"Tell me about the
  business — what you do, what you charge, how busy you are, what's working."* Harvest
  every lever the answer touches. "I charge $60 a lesson, do maybe 10 a week, mostly
  word of mouth" just answered price, volume, AND the acquisition channel — credit all
  of them, never re-ask what they already told you.
- **Pre-fill from the web.** If they have a website, Google listing, or booking page,
  ask for it early and pull prices, services, and hours from it. Then *confirm* instead
  of asking: "Your site says $60/lesson — still right?" A confirmation costs the owner
  almost nothing; a question costs attention.
- **Then ask only the gaps**, most important first: outcome → ⭐ constraint → bank
  anchor → price/volume → costs → capital/hours. Recap what you have as you go so they
  can correct instead of recite.
- **The extras are opportunistic-only.** Never walk the extras list top to bottom —
  record them when they come up naturally, or skip them. `[ASK]` is always an acceptable
  answer; a tired owner gives worse numbers than a research pass does.

## Q0 — New, existing, or existing-plus-new? (ask first — it forks everything)
> "Are we starting a brand-new idea, growing something you already run, or adding a new
> offering to a business you already run?"

- **Existing** → real present numbers to anchor on. The model will reproduce the present
  (rule 3). The core question becomes *"what's the constraint to growth?"*
- **New** → no present to reproduce. Expect more `[BENCHMARK]` and `[ASK]`. The core
  question becomes *"is this viable, and what has to be true for it to work?"*
- **Existing + new offering** (a brake shop adding fleet accounts, a coach adding camps)
  → run the Existing track on the current core business, then the three New-idea
  questions scoped to the new offering only. They'll be modeled as **separate lines** —
  the core reproduced against its anchor, the new line with its own break-even.

## Q1 — The outcome (the north star)
> "Picture 3 years out. What does this business look like, and what would make you say
> it actually worked?"

Push past vague. "More money" → *"How much a month would feel like a win?"* "Bigger" →
*"Bigger how — more customers, a second location, hiring, or the same size but easier?"*
Everything downstream serves this answer. Record it at the top of `intake/interview.md`.

## The core levers
Adapt the wording to their business. Get a number or a rough range for each; `[ASK]`
anything they can't answer.

1. **Unit price** — "What do you charge for a typical [job/session/order]? Cheapest and
   most expensive version?" Follow up: **"When did you last raise prices, and what
   happened?"** (Often the cheapest growth move on the whole board — no marketing, no
   hire. "Never, I'm scared to" vs. "last year, nobody blinked" is hugely diagnostic.)
2. **Volume — normal and peak** — "How many do you do in a normal week? Your busiest ever?"
   (Normal = the engine. Peak = the ceiling a growth move gets measured against.)
3. **Cost of delivery (COGS)** — "Of what a customer pays, how much goes to parts/materials/
   supplies for that one job?" (dollars or "about a third" both fine)
4. **Fixed costs** — "What does it cost to keep the doors open each month, regardless of
   how busy you are — rent, vehicle, insurance, tools, subscriptions?"
5. ⭐ **The constraint** — "Do you ever turn people away, run a waitlist, or make people
   wait a week or more? How often?" *(THE most important question. It reveals whether the
   bottleneck is demand or capacity — which decides whether the growth move is marketing
   or more hands. Don't let them shrug it off; a gut feel counts. For a business without
   a capacity limit, the constraint is whatever stops the next sale: traffic, conversion,
   stock.)*

   **If demand-bound** ("I never turn anyone away — I need more customers"), three
   mandatory follow-ups — for a demand-bound business these ARE the plan:
   - "Where did your last 10 customers actually come from?"
   - "What share of customers come back, or send you someone?"
   - "What have you tried to get customers, and what did it cost?"

6. **Growth capital** — "If you wanted to grow, how much of your own money could you put
   in without it stressing you out?" (separates "buy outright" from "finance it" — not a
   commitment)
7. **Owner hours** *(existing businesses — core, not optional)* — "Real talk, how many
   hours a week are you actually working in this business?" (The model prices your labor
   with this — a plan that pays you $0/hour is a lie.)

## Existing businesses — the bank-account anchor (mandatory)
> "Bank-account truth: over the last 3 months, roughly what actually landed in your
> pocket per month? And rough total revenue over the last year?"

This is deliberately independent of the levers. The model must reproduce this number
(rule 3); the gap between the levers and the bank account is usually the most useful
thing the whole process finds. Record it as **Actual take-home (anchor)**.

## More if they're talkative
8. Labor — "If you hired help, what would you pay them? A trainee?"
9. Job mix — "What does a typical job include? What's the upsell?"
10. Seasonality — "When are you dead? When are you slammed?"
11. Financing appetite — "Would you take a loan to grow, or do you hate that idea?"
12. Repeat/fleet accounts — "Any customers who come back a lot, or need you for many
    units at once?" (repeat revenue is the cheapest way to fill new capacity)

## For a NEW idea (or the new offering in a hybrid), also ask
- "Who exactly is the customer, and what do they do today instead of using you?"
- "What makes someone pick you over what already exists?"
- "What's the one assumption that, if wrong, sinks the whole thing?" (research targets this)

When done, confirm the outcome and the levers back to them in one short recap, update
`intake/STATE.md`, then move to `02-research.md`.
