## 1. The outcome
**5 steady weekly clients bringing in $100–200/week, within 3 years.**
The good news up front: you're closer than it feels. Just **repricing to $40** puts your
*proven* peak (5 clients / 3–4 sessions a week [owner-stated]) at **~$140/week — squarely
inside your goal** — with zero new clients. Reaching the **$200/week top** takes one more
step: getting all 5 clients into a *weekly* slot (5 sessions/week), which is slightly more
frequency than you've shown before. Clients and sessions aren't the same thing — the plan
is honest about which lever moves which.

## 3. The constraint — it's not what it looks like
It feels like a demand problem ("I need more customers"). The research says the deeper
problem is **keeping** clients through the year. You **acquired ~5 clients fine** — the
business didn't break on getting customers, it broke on **continuity**: winter + your own
season emptied the calendar and you had to rebuild from scratch.

Two forces make continuity the real constraint:
1. **Winter kills an outdoor-only model.** Philadelphia stays below freezing all day for
   much of **Dec–March** [VERIFIED — NOAA normals] — which is *peak* school-basketball
   season and exactly when families most want training. An indoor competitor in Bala
   Cynwyd trains year-round [VERIFIED — Danny Cooper Basketball].
2. **Your own season pulls you out** at the same time [owner-stated].

**So the plan is mostly about plugging the leak, not just filling the top of the bucket.**
Get back to 5 clients, charge properly, and — the hard part — *hold them across winter.*

- Your target towns (Lower Merion) hold **~15,500 kids under 18** [VERIFIED — Census] with

- **Basketball is the #1 youth sport**, 41.9% of kids 6–17 play [VERIFIED — Aspen/Project
  Play] → roughly **6,500 basketball-playing kids** in reach. You need **5**. That's
  **~0.08%** of the pool.
- Private-pay training skews toward higher-income families (a 20.2-point gap) [VERIFIED],

- **The wedge:** the local market is **price-opaque** — competitors list "Contact for
  price" [VERIFIED — Lessons.com]. You can compete on being *easy, trusted, and
  mobile-to-them*, and you're currently the cheapest by a wide margin (which is a problem,
  not an advantage — see §5).
- **Your price is below the floor:** private 1-on-1 runs **$30–$150/hr** in Philadelphia,
  beginner tier **$30–$60** [VERIFIED — Lessons.com / TeachMe.To]. At $25 you undercut even
  the beginner floor.

## 6. The path — moves tied to triggers, not dates
**Move 1 — Fix the price (do first; costs nothing).**
Raise 1-on-1 to **$40**, paired to **$60** ($30/kid). Tell existing families 2–3 weeks
ahead; grandfather your most loyal 1–2 if you want. *Trigger: now.* If anyone balks, your
"$30 beginner floor" data says you're still cheap [VERIFIED].

**Move 2 — Become bookable & trustworthy (this week).**
- Add a **one-tap booking link** (your site currently only says "text me" [VERIFIED] —
  every click that doesn't text you is lost).
- Get your **PA clearances** (Child Abuse $13, State Police $10, FBI ~$27.50 — often
  waived first-time; ~**$50 total** [VERIFIED]) and **display them on your About page**,
  which currently shows none [VERIFIED]. For Main Line parents, "cleared & background-checked"
  is a closing argument. *Trigger: before your next ad dollar.*
- **Reactivate your Google Business Profile** link (the Ads warning flagged a broken
  connection) — it's your best free local channel.

**Move 3 — Rebuild to 5 clients (your proven number).**
Run **Facebook + Google Ads** at ~$90/mo total, lead with the booking link and clearances.
*Trigger: once Moves 1–2 are done.* Expected cost **~$40–130/client** (Facebook ~$19/lead
[VERIFIED] × your close rate; Google ~$41 observed but n=1 [owner data]) — ~4 clients ≈
**$160–400 total, spread over months.** *Stop-and-rethink trigger:* if 60 clicks produce
**0** inquiries, the landing page — not the ad — is the problem; fix the page before spending more.

**Move 4 — Solve winter *before* it arrives (the make-or-break move).**
You picked client homes/driveway hoops and Philly parks-with-permit as your venues — good,
those dodge the Lower Merion park ban [VERIFIED]. For **Dec–March**, push families toward
**their own driveway/garage hoops** or a **weekend indoor slot**, and — *trigger: when you
hit 5 steady clients* — approach one **indoor facility** (gym, church, rec center) about a
few paid winter hours. This is the one gap that has already beaten you once; treat it as
the priority, not an afterthought.

**Move 5 — Plug your own-season hole.**
*Trigger: 4 weeks before your season starts.* Line up a **temporary reduced schedule**
(early mornings / weekends) or a **trusted sub** so clients don't churn to a year-round
competitor. Keeping a regular client is worth far more than winning a new one — kids
typically quit their sport by age 11 [VERIFIED], so a client you lose over the winter is
often gone for good.

## 7. Risks & honest caveats
- **Venue (the YELLOW risk).** Lower Merion Township bars solo for-profit coaching in its
  public parks [VERIFIED — township code] — that's your whole advertised area. Your chosen
  fix (client homes/private hoops + Philly permits) works, but running informal public-park
  sessions in those towns is technically against the ordinance (rarely enforced, but real).
  Lean on private/home venues as the default.
- **Seasonality + your season (the other YELLOW risk).** An outdoor model has a hard hole
  Dec–March that overlaps your own season — the exact thing that collapsed the business
  last year. Move 4 and Move 5 exist for this. If you skip them, expect the same drop.
- **Churn.** You train the youngest kids — the highest-churn group; basketball is also the
  lowest-spend youth sport ($427/yr/family [VERIFIED]). Retention (packages, consistent
  weekly slots) matters more than a flashy funnel.
- **The single fact that would have flipped this to RED:** if Philadelphia parks *also*
  refused individual paid-instruction permits **and** no home/private-court path existed,
  you'd have a zero-cost service with nowhere legal to operate. Philadelphia **does** issue
  those permits (~10-day approval [VERIFIED]) and private venues exist — so it stays YELLOW,
  not RED.
- **Your time barely clears its own worth — until you batch lessons.** Priced against the
  *all-coaches* Philadelphia wage (~$32.86/hr [VERIFIED — BLS], which includes salaried
  school/college coaches, so it overstates a beginner youth trainer's real alternative
  wage), a single stand-alone lesson with ~50 min of travel doesn't cover that labor even
  at $40. The fix is **batching 2–3 lessons per park trip** so travel is shared — that's
  what lifts your effective pay toward market. It's the one operational habit the whole
  model leans on, and you haven't done it yet, so treat it as a real to-do, not a given.
- **One source we couldn't double-check:** the $68,350 / $32.86-hr coach wage is from the
  BLS wage tables, but BLS blocks automated re-fetching, so it wasn't independently
  re-confirmed on review. It doesn't change the verdict (your ~100% cash margin does), but
  verify it yourself at bls.gov before leaning on the "you're underpaid vs. market" line.
- **What would change the plan:** if your "25 min travel" was round-trip (not each way),
  your effective wage is higher and batching matters less; if your true lead→client close
  rate is low, ad spend needs the landing-page fix (Move 2) first.
